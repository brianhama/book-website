🌍 Planetary Health · One Health

Where Healthcare
Meets Ecology

Human health and the living planet are a single, looped system. Ecosystems keep us well — and the way we deliver care, in turn, reshapes those ecosystems. Here is that intersection, in data.

The big idea

There is no human health on a sick planet.

Medicine tends to treat the body as if it ended at the skin. But clean air, safe water, a stable climate, biodiverse landscapes, and the animals we live alongside are all upstream of whether people get sick or stay well. That is the premise of One Health and Planetary Health: human, animal, and ecosystem health are one interdependent system.

The relationship runs both directions. Degraded ecosystems drive disease — through pollution, heat, and pathogens that spill over from wildlife. And health care itself is a heavy industry: if the global health sector were a country, it would be the planet's fifth-largest climate polluter. The visualizations below trace that loop.

Care has a carbon footprint

If health care were a country, it would be the 5th-largest emitter

The global health sector produces about 2.0 gigatonnes of CO₂e a year — 4.4% of net global emissions. Here is where those emissions come from.

    Scopes follow the Greenhouse Gas Protocol: Scope 1 is direct emissions (facilities, vehicles), Scope 2 is purchased energy, and Scope 3 is the supply chain (pharmaceuticals, devices, food, transport) — by far the largest share.

    Ecology → Health

    A warming world, on the move

    As the climate shifts, mosquito-borne disease climbs

    Dengue cases reported to the WHO have risen roughly 13-fold this century as warming and urbanization expand the range of the Aedes mosquito.

    Reported cases reflect both true spread and improving surveillance, but the trend tracks the expanding climatic suitability for transmission. Dengue is now the fastest-spreading vector-borne disease in the world.

    Source: World Health Organization dengue surveillance; Lancet Countdown on Health and Climate Change.

    Closing the loop

    The intersection, in three moves

    • Ecosystems are upstream of health. Pollution, unsafe water, lead, and heat quietly account for roughly one in four deaths — more than any single disease.
    • Health care is upstream of ecosystems. The sector built to heal is also a top-five global polluter, most of it hidden in the supply chain.
    • The fixes are shared. Cleaner energy, resilient food and water, protected habitat, and low-carbon care are simultaneously climate policy and health policy. Treating them as one system is the whole point of planetary health.
